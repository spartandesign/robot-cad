Quick Laser Parts in TinkerCAD

Onshape is where the robot lives. But for a quick flat part, like a pit sign, a drill jig, a spacer, or a field-element mockup, TinkerCAD can export an SVG file that both of our lasers read directly. This module teaches the TinkerCAD tricks that make laser files come out right.

🛠️ PLTW ProjectModule 6 · Week 15

A PLTW Project asks you to use skills you already have to make something real, with clear requirements and limits.

🎯 Your goal

  • I can build an exact-size flat part in TinkerCAD, in millimeters.
  • I can export an SVG that has every outline I need, and nothing extra.
  • I can pick the right laser for the material and set up the file for it.

🧰 You’ll need

  • TinkerCAD (browser, or the iPad app)
  • Calipers
  • The class laser test card (power and speed for each material)
  • Your coach runs the laser
  • The workplane tool on this page

⏳ Time and limits

  • 2 weeks of class build time (Weeks 14–15), plus cut time. Homework: the two TinkerCAD lessons on this page.
  • Ador: wood, plywood, card, and dark or black acrylic only. LC40: the only machine for clear or colored acrylic.
  • Never laser polycarbonate (Lexan).
  • Anything that bolts onto the competition robot must follow the current game manual.
  • Keep it smaller than the laser bed, and name the file with your team and part.

Lesson 1 (homework): exact sizes in millimeters

TinkerCAD works in millimeters. The VEX pattern is 0.5 in = 12.7 mm. A #8 screw clearance hole is 0.170 in = 4.32 mm.

Goal: a plate at an exact size

Size a box

Drag a Box onto the workplane. Click its dimensions and type 76.2 × 50.8 mm, 3 mm tall. That’s 6 × 4 VEX holes.

✅ Check it: the ruler shows 76.2 and 50.8.

Goal: holes that will cut

Add Hole cylinders

Drag a Cylinder in and set it to Hole. Size it to your drawn hole (see the kerf step below). Make it taller than the plate.

✅ Check it: the cylinder looks see-through and striped.

Goal: holes on the pattern

Place them exactly

Place hole centers 12.7 mm apart. Use the ruler, or type the X and Y positions. Then select everything, Align, and Group.

✅ Check it: after grouping, you see real holes through the plate.

Kerf: draw holes a little smaller

The laser burns away a thin strip, so holes come out bigger. Draw them smaller by the kerf from your test card: drawn = needed − kerf. For example, 4.32 − 0.20 = 4.12 mm. Same rule as Module 5, now in millimeters.

Lesson 2 (homework): export an SVG the laser can use

TinkerCAD exports the outline of your design where it crosses the workplane, as thin red cut lines. Anything floating above the workplane doesn’t make it into the file. Try it:

Goal: everything on the workplane

Drop it down

Select your part and press D (or use the Drop button) so it sits on the workplane.

✅ Check it: nothing is floating.

Goal: the right shapes in the file

Export the SVG

Click Export. Under Include, choose everything or just the selected shapes. Under For Laser Cutting, choose SVG.

✅ Check it: the file downloads. Name it like Team2822X_PitSign.svg.

Goal: the right size in the laser

Check it in the laser software

Ador: import the SVG into Beam Studio and choose Single Layer. LC40: upload it in the LC40 software. Either way, check that the part measures the size you made it before anyone cuts.

✅ Check it: the 76.2 mm plate measures 76.2 mm in the laser software.

Goal: cut settings that work

Set cut and engrave

The SVG only holds cut lines. Your coach sets power and speed from the class test card. To mark a team number without cutting it out, set that part to engrave in the laser software.

✅ Check it: your coach approves the settings and runs the cut.

⚠️ Letters fall apart

If you cut text all the way through, the middles of letters like A, B, D, O, P, R, 0, 4, 6, 8, and 9 fall out. Use a stencil-style font, or engrave the text instead of cutting it.
