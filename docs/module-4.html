Mates that move

  • Revolute: spins around one axis. Use it for arm pivots, wheels, and claw fingers.
  • Slider: moves along one line. Use it for linear lifts and telescoping parts.
  • Limits: set the top and bottom of the motion, so the CAD can’t do what the real robot can’t.

The parallel four-bar lift

A four-bar is four links joined at four pivots: the tower, two arms, and the top link. When the two arms are the same length and the top link matches the tower, it’s a parallel four-bar. The top link stays at the same angle the whole way up, so a claw or intake on it stays level. That’s why so many VEX lifts use it.

Try it: the four-bar

Press Play. Then change the link lengths and watch the top link tilt.

Interference: the silent robot-killer

A lift that looks fine at the bottom can crash into the frame halfway up. In CAD you can check every position for free. Move the mechanism to the bottom, middle, and top, and look for parts in the same space.

Keep it low. Heavy parts high up make a robot tip. When you can, mount motors and batteries low.

Model a lift

Goal: the lift’s skeleton

Place the tower and arms

On your Module 1 frame, insert a tower and two equal arms from the library. Match your sketch.

✅ Check it: both arms are the same length, in holes.

Goal: it moves

Mate the pivots

Add revolute mates at all four pivots.

✅ Check it: drag an arm. The whole four-bar moves together.

Goal: it stops where the robot stops

Add limits

Edit the bottom pivot’s mate. Turn on limits and set the lowest and highest angles.

✅ Check it: you can’t drag past the top or bottom.

Goal: no crashes

Animate and check

Animate the lift. Pause at the bottom, middle, and top. Look for overlaps, and measure the highest reach.

✅ Check it: no overlaps anywhere, and the reach is in your notebook.

📓 For your engineering notebook

  • Screenshots at the bottom and top, with the reach in inches.
  • Your limit angles, and why you chose them.
  • Any interference you found, and how you fixed it.
