🧩 PLTW ActivityModule 5 · set by your coach

A PLTW Activity teaches a skill step by step. Follow the steps closely; the goal is to learn the tool.

🎯 Your goal

  • I can model a flat plate with holes on the 0.5 in pattern.
  • I can size laser-cut holes using the measured kerf.
  • I can export a laser file the right way for each of our lasers.

🧰 You’ll need

  • Onshape and calipers
  • A sample of the sheet material (measure its thickness)
  • Your coach runs the laser
  • The hole calculator on this page

⏳ Time and limits

  • 2 weeks of class build time (Weeks 12–13), plus cut time and the Onshape homework below.
  • Your coach decides which materials go in the laser. Never laser polycarbonate (Lexan): it cuts badly and gives off harmful fumes.
  • Check the current game manual for which materials are legal on the robot.
  • Measure the kerf on a test cut before trusting any hole size.

Step-by-step plan

Follow the steps in order. The minutes are a guide. Check off each checkpoint before you move on.

Build session 1

  1. 10 minWatch the video.
  2. 15 minCaliper the sheet thickness. Get the kerf from a test cut (or your class kerf card).
  3. 15 minUse the hole calculator to find your drawn hole size.
  4. 20 minModel the plate: sketch, holes on #pitch, extrude to the measured thickness.

✔ Checkpoint: A plate model with compensated holes.

Build session 2

  1. 10 minExport the laser file: DXF for the Ador, or a 1:1 PDF drawing for the LC40. Name it with your team and part.
  2. 10 minYour coach cuts it.
  3. 20 minTest-fit it on the robot with real screws. Log the fit.
  4. 20 minAdjust if needed. Take the self-check.

✔ Checkpoint: A cut plate that bolts on, with the fit logged.

When to laser-cut

A laser is great for flat shapes with lots of holes: mounting plates, spacers, sensor guards, and quick prototypes. Your coach decides what material goes in the laser. Never laser polycarbonate (Lexan): it cuts badly and gives off harmful fumes. And always check the current game manual for which materials are allowed on the robot.

Kerf: laser holes come out big

The beam burns away a thin strip, called the kerf. A cut-out part comes out a little smaller than drawn, and a hole comes out a little bigger. So draw holes smaller by the kerf: drawn hole = hole you need − kerf. Measure your kerf on a test cut. Every material and machine is different.

Goal: a file the laser can read

Export for your laser

Flux Ador: right-click the flat face and choose Export as DXF/DWG. Beam Studio opens DXF files. Dremel LC40: its software takes SVG or PDF, not DXF, so make a 1:1 drawing of the plate (no border or title block) and export it as PDF. For simple flat parts, you can also rebuild the plate in TinkerCAD and export an SVG (Module 6). Name files like Team2822X_PlateName.

✅ Check it: your coach opens it and it’s the right size.
