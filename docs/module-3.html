Gears and Drivetrains

Speed or strength? Every drivetrain, lift, and intake is a trade between the two. This module turns gear ratios into numbers you can design with, then has you model a gear train that really meshes in Onshape.

Every drivetrain, lift, and intake trades speed for strength. Gear ratios turn that trade into numbers. VEX writes a gear ratio as driven to driving: output teeth over input teeth. A twelve tooth gear on the motor, driving a sixty tooth gear, is sixty to twelve. That's five to one. The output turns five times slower, with five times the torque. The motor's cartridge sets the starting speed: red is one hundred RPM, green is two hundred, and blue is six hundred. Red for torque, green for drivetrains, and blue for speed. To get robot speed, multiply RPM by pi and the wheel diameter, then divide by sixty. Two hundred RPM on a three and a quarter inch wheel is about thirty-four inches per second. But faster isn't always better. Less torque makes a robot harder to control. In Onshape, give each gear a revolute mate. Then add a gear relation, so turning one turns the other. The one number: gear ratio

VEX writes a gear ratio as driven : driving, output teeth over input teeth. A 12-tooth gear on the motor driving a 60-tooth gear is 60 : 12 = 5 : 1.

  • Speed: output RPM = motor RPM ÷ ratio.
  • Torque: output torque = motor torque × ratio.
  • Compound gears (two stages on shared shafts) multiply: 5 : 1 then 3 : 1 is 15 : 1.

The V5 Smart Motor’s cartridge sets the starting speed: red 100 RPM (torque), green 200 RPM (drivetrains), and blue 600 RPM (speed).

From RPM to robot speed

A wheel moves one circumference per turn. Wheel speed in inches per second = RPM × π × wheel diameter ÷ 60. Divide by 12 for feet per second. Measure your own wheels; common V5 sizes are 2.75, 3.25, and 4 in.

Faster isn’t always better. More speed means less torque and a harder robot to control. Pick the ratio that does the job.

Model a gear train

Goal: the right parts

Insert gears and shafts

In an Assembly, insert your two gears and two shafts from the V5 library, plus the metal they mount on.

✅ Check it: the tooth counts match your notebook.

Goal: gears that can spin

Add revolute mates

Give each gear a revolute mate on its shaft axis.

✅ Check it: drag each gear. It spins on its own shaft.

Goal: gears that turn together

Add a gear relation

Choose Gear relation, select both revolute mates, and enter your ratio.

✅ Check it: turn one gear. The other turns the opposite way at the right speed.

Goal: a mesh that works in metal

Check the center distance

From the top view, zoom in on the teeth. They should touch without overlapping, and both shafts should pass through real holes.

✅ Check it: no overlap and no gap. If the gears don’t land on holes, try a different gear pair or mounting spot.
