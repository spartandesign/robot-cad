🏁 PLTW ProblemModule 7 · showcase in Week 20 (Jun 1–4)

A PLTW Problem is open-ended. Your team defines the solution within the constraints, tests it, and improves it.

🎯 Your goal

  • We can define a design problem from the current game manual and our strategy.
  • We can design a subsystem in CAD using library and custom parts.
  • We can review, build, test, and improve it, and record it in our notebook.

🧰 You’ll need

  • The current V5RC game manual
  • Onshape, the V5 library, and Modules 1–6 skills
  • Decision matrix and design brief pages
  • Robot parts, and print or laser time from your coach
  • The design review checklist on this page

⏳ Time and limits

  • 5 weeks of class build time (Weeks 16–20), plus the Onshape homework below.
  • The subsystem must follow the current game manual (size, legal parts, custom-part rules).
  • Each squad member owns a piece, on their own branch.
  • Save a named version for every revision.

Step-by-step plan

Follow the steps in order. The minutes are a guide. Check off each checkpoint before you move on.

Build session 1

  1. 15 minRead the game manual sections that matter. List the jobs your subsystem must do.
  2. 20 minWrite the design brief: problem, requirements, and limits.
  3. 25 minBrainstorm three ideas. Use a decision matrix to choose one.

✔ Checkpoint: Design brief and decision matrix in the notebook.

Build session 2

  1. 10 minSplit the work. Make branches.
  2. 40 minModel it in CAD: library parts plus any custom printed or laser parts.
  3. 10 minMerge. Check fit inside the robot assembly.

✔ Checkpoint: A merged subsystem model.

Build session 3

  1. 20 minDesign review: walk through the checklist with your coach or another squad.
  2. 25 minFix what the review found. Save a version (v2).
  3. 15 minSubmit any print or laser files.

✔ Checkpoint: Review passed. Parts ordered or printing.

Build session 4

  1. 30 minBuild it from the CAD.
  2. 20 minTest it against your requirements. Log the results.
  3. 10 minPlan the next revision. Update the notebook.

✔ Checkpoint: A built, tested subsystem, with results and next steps logged.

Onshape is where our robot lives. But for a quick flat part, like a pit sign, a drill jig, or a spacer, TinkerCAD is faster. It exports an S V G file that both of our lasers can read. TinkerCAD works in millimeters. The VEX hole spacing, half an inch, is twelve point seven millimeters. A screw clearance hole is about four point three millimeters. Draw it a little smaller, by the kerf, because laser holes come out big. Here's the big idea. TinkerCAD exports the outline where your design crosses the workplane. If your part is floating above it, the file comes out empty. Press D to drop it down, then export. Click Export. Under For Laser Cutting, choose S V G. You get thin red cut lines, in millimeters. The Ador uses Beam Studio, for wood, plywood, card, and dark acrylic. The LC40 opens its software in a web browser, and it's the only one for clear or colored acrylic. Either way, check the part's size before anyone cuts. One last trick. If you cut letters all the way through, their middles fall out. Engrave the text instead, or use a stencil font.

The problem

This is a PLTW-style Problem: open-ended, with real constraints. Your squad chooses a subsystem and decides what “success” means, based on the game and your strategy. Then you prove it with tests.

Run the design process

Goal: a clear problem

Define it from the game manual

Read the parts of the current game manual that affect your subsystem: size limits, legal parts, and how points are scored. Write a design brief: the problem, what it must do, and its limits.

✅ Check it: every requirement in your brief ties to the manual or your strategy.

Goal: a choice you can defend

Brainstorm and decide

Sketch at least three ideas. Score them in a decision matrix against your requirements.

✅ Check it: the winner has the best score, and you can explain why.

Goal: a model that fits

Design it in CAD

Each member models a piece on their own branch, using library parts plus any custom printed or laser parts from Modules 3, 5, and 6. Merge into the robot assembly.

✅ Check it: no interference through the full motion (Module 4), and your gear math is done (Module 2).

Goal: fresh eyes

Hold a design review

Walk through the checklist below with your coach or another squad. Fix what they find, and save a named version.

✅ Check it: every checklist item is checked.

Goal: proof it works

Build, test, improve

Build it from the CAD. Test it against each requirement and record the results. Plan the next version.

✅ Check it: your test log shows numbers, not just “it works.”

Try it: design review checklist

📓 For your engineering notebook

  • Design brief, with game-manual rule numbers.
  • Decision matrix and sketches of every idea.
  • CAD screenshots or drawings of the chosen design, with its version name.
  • The design review notes and what you changed.
  • Test results, with numbers and photos, plus the next steps.
