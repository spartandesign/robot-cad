Getting Started

Laptop or iPad?

You can do this course on a laptop or an iPad. Some jobs are easier on one than the other. This page shows how to do each job on both. Every module also has a short “Laptop or iPad?” table.

Which device for which job

Onshape modeling (parts, assemblies)✅ Best with a mouse✅ Works in the Onshape app

Laptop tip: plug in a mouse. A trackpad works, but rotating and panning are much harder. iPad tip: an Apple Pencil makes picking small lines easier, and you can use Split View to keep the lesson guide open next to Onshape.

Screenshots

  • Windows laptop: Win + Shift + S, then drag a box. Paste it onto your notebook slide.
  • Chromebook: Ctrl + Show Windows for the whole screen, or Ctrl + Shift + Show Windows for part of it.
  • iPad: press the top button and volume up together. The picture goes to Photos.

Google Slides, Google Docs, and Canvas

  • Your notebook (Google Slides) and Docs: on a laptop, use the browser. On iPad, use the Google Slides and Google Docs apps: insert photos straight from the Camera, and type in the tables. Sketch on paper and add a photo.
  • Canvas: on a laptop, use the browser. On iPad, use the Canvas Student app. To record a spoken reflection, choose Media (or Record) when you submit.

Reading and listening help

  • Every chapter has a 🔊 Listen player. Use Slower if it’s too fast.
  • In Canvas, the Immersive Reader button reads a page aloud and highlights the words.
  • iPad: Settings → Accessibility → Spoken Content → Speak Screen. Then swipe down with two fingers from the top to hear any page.
  • Laptop: in Microsoft Edge, right-click the page and choose Read aloud.
  • Use headphones for videos and audio, so the room stays quiet.

If something won’t work

  • Onshape saves automatically, so nothing is lost if your device dies. Charge it before class.
  • If a step is too hard on iPad, switch to a laptop for that step, or ask a laptop teammate to do it while you watch.
  • Still stuck? Write down what you tried and ask your teacher.
